THE BRIEF
Good product.
Wrong story.
Genesis Photobooks had quality on their side from day one. What they didn't have was a way to show it. They came to us for photography and video. They left with something much bigger — a product and a visual identity that the market had never seen before.
Genesis Photobooks is a Bengaluru-based photo album company just finding their footing in a crowded market. Their catalogue had range — different sizes, materials, finishes, aesthetic directions — but nothing was cohesive, and nothing was commanding the price their craft deserved. Albums that should have been selling for ₹25,000 were going for ₹2,500, not because they weren't worth more, but because nothing about how they were presented said they were. The photography was functional. The product design was scattered. And without a clear visual language, they were competing on price in a market where the real opportunity was to compete on prestige.
WHAT WAS HOLDING THEM BACK
The Gap Between Quality and Perception
01
No Product Cohesion
A wide range of albums with different materials, sizes and aesthetics — but no unifying design language to tie them together as a premium brand.
02
Commodity Positioning
Without intentional visual identity, the products were being evaluated on price alone — competing with mass-market albums instead of standing apart from them.
03
Photography That Didn't Sell
The existing product imagery was flat and functional. It showed what the albums looked like. It didn't communicate what owning one felt like.
WHAT WE DID
We Started With the Product. Then We Shot It.
Most studios show up with a camera. We showed up with questions. Before a single frame was shot, we rebuilt what Genesis was selling — then made sure the world could see it.
01
Product Design Consultation
We sat with the Genesis team and went through every album in their range. For each one, we made decisions on material weight, cover finish, size proportions, and colour palette — not to redesign their products, but to make intentional choices that justified a premium price point.
02
Visual Identity Direction
We defined a visual language for Genesis — the environments, textures, lighting moods, and styling contexts that their albums would live in. Warm natural surfaces, editorial framing, lifestyle context. Every visual decision was made to communicate one thing: luxury.
03
Full Catalogue Photography
Shot the entire product range with precision — hero shots, detail close-ups, lifestyle context, and multi-angle coverage for every SKU. Each image was designed to make the viewer feel the weight of the cover and the texture of the pages without touching them.
04
Product Videos
Produced short-form product videos for each album — slow, deliberate, cinematic. The kind of content that stops a scroll. Shot to show the tactile experience of the product: how it opens, how pages turn, how light falls on the cover.
THE OUTCOME
Same product.
Ten times the price.
Albums that sold for ₹2,500–₹3,000 now sell for ₹25,000–₹30,000. Clients buy without hesitation.
Genesis Photobooks went to market with a catalogue that looked nothing like what the category expected. The product design decisions made the albums feel considered and rare. The photography made them feel aspirational. The videos made them feel alive.
The result was a 10x jump in price point — not through a rebrand, not through new products, but through a fundamental shift in how the existing range was designed, positioned, and presented. Clients who now encounter Genesis don't ask why the albums cost ₹30,000. They ask how to order.
That's what happens when the product and the story around it finally match.
We didn't change what they made. We changed what people saw when they looked at it.
