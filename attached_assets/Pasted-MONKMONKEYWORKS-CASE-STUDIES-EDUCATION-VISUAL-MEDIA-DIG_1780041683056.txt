DAYS OF PRIOR REPUTATION
THE BRIEF
Premium school.
No track record.
Aurralis Montessori had done everything right on the inside — the infrastructure, the philosophy, the environment. What they didn't have was a single parent who'd heard of them. They needed trust built from nothing, fast.
Aurralis Montessori launched in Basavanagudi, Bengaluru as a premium early childhood education space for toddlers to 3-year-olds, with fees ranging from ₹1.1 to ₹1.5 lakh per year. The school had invested heavily in its physical space — thoughtful infrastructure, a genuine Montessori environment, and a clear premium positioning. But on day one, they were invisible. No word of mouth. No alumni. No reputation in the neighbourhood. Just 40 empty seats and a deadline to fill them before the academic year began. Their fear wasn't the product. Their fear was that no parent would ever find out it existed.
THE REAL PROBLEM
Parents Don't Trust. They Verify.
Selling a premium school to parents of toddlers is one of the hardest marketing challenges there is. Parents aren't buying a product — they're making a decision about their child's first experience of the world outside home. Price alone doesn't build confidence. Ads alone don't build trust. What parents need to see is evidence — that the environment is safe, that the energy is right, that the people running it genuinely care. That evidence has to be felt, not stated. Which is exactly why visual media wasn't a nice-to-have here. It was the entire strategy.
WHAT THEY WERE UP AGAINST

Brand new school with no existing parent community or word-of-mouth pipeline

Premium fee structure in a market full of established, cheaper alternatives

Target audience — parents of toddlers — among the most cautious buyers in any category

A fixed deadline — seats had to be filled before the academic year opened
WHAT WE DID
Show the Life. Then Run the Ads.
We didn't lead with promotions. We led with proof. Every piece of content we created was designed to answer one question a parent was silently asking: "Is this a place I'd trust with my child?"
01
Day-in-the-Life Visual Content
We produced natural, documentary-style content showing the actual life of children inside Aurralis — how they move through the space, how teachers engage with them, the texture of a real Montessori day. Not staged. Not promotional. Just honest, beautifully shot evidence that the environment was everything it claimed to be.
02
Event & Summer Camp Coverage
Aurralis was already planning a summer camp — we turned it into a lead generation engine. We covered the camp with high-quality photo and video content that gave parents who hadn't enrolled yet a window into the school's world. Seeing real children thriving inside the space did more than any ad copy ever could.
03
Curated Event Strategy
We helped curate events that gave prospective parents a physical reason to visit — not a sales pitch, but an experience. Getting a parent through the door of the school once is worth more than a hundred impressions on a feed. The space itself was the closer.
04
Instagram & Google Ad Campaigns
With strong visual content in hand, our digital marketing team ran targeted campaigns on Instagram and Google — reaching parents of toddlers in Basavanagudi and surrounding areas. The content did the trust-building. The ads did the reach. Together they created a pipeline that converted.
THE OUTCOME
75% filled.
Before they opened.
30 out of 40 seats filled in 45 days. A brand new school. No prior reputation. Premium fees. Zero hesitation from parents.
REVENUE IMPACT
₹33L — ₹45L
Projected annual fee revenue from 30 enrolled families at ₹1.1L–₹1.5L per seat. This is what 45 days of the right content and the right ads unlocked for a school that didn't exist in the market two months earlier.
In 45 days, Aurralis Montessori went from an empty building with a great philosophy to a school that was 75% capacity-filled before it had served a single full term. The remaining 10 seats were still in conversation at the close of the engagement — the pipeline didn't stop when we did.
What made this work wasn't the ad spend. It was the content that made the ads worth clicking. Parents who came through the funnel had already seen the school through our visual media — they arrived informed, warm, and largely pre-sold. The conversion happened because trust had been built before the first enquiry call.
For a school entering a market with no reputation and a premium price point, filling 75% of capacity in 45 days is not a typical outcome. It happened because the product was genuinely good — and for the first time, the world could see it.
Parents don't buy schools. They buy the feeling that their child will be okay.
